# Grade 8 Mathematics Curriculum Guide: Numbers, Fractions & Basic Algebra

## Chapter 1: Basic Arithmetic Operations & Properties

### Section 1.1: Addition and Subtraction Fundamentals
Addition is the process of combining two or more numbers to calculate their total sum.
Subtraction is the operation of taking one number away from another to find the difference.

Properties of Addition:
1. Commutative Property: a + b = b + a (e.g., 5 + 3 = 3 + 5 = 8)
2. Associative Property: (a + b) + c = a + (b + c)
3. Identity Element: a + 0 = a

Common Misconception: Subtracting a negative number is equivalent to adding its positive counterpart. For example, 10 - (-4) = 10 + 4 = 14.

### Section 1.2: Multiplication & Repeated Addition
Multiplication is repeated addition of equal groups.
Product = Multiplicand × Multiplier.
For example, 4 × 7 means adding 7 four times: 7 + 7 + 7 + 7 = 28.

Properties of Multiplication:
1. Distributive Property: a × (b + c) = (a × b) + (a × c)
2. Identity Element: a × 1 = a
3. Zero Property: a × 0 = 0

### Section 1.3: Division and Inverse Multiplication
Division is the inverse operation of multiplication. It splits a quantity into equal parts or groups.
Dividend ÷ Divisor = Quotient (with Remainder if not exact).
Rule: Division by zero is undefined.

## Chapter 2: Rational Numbers & Fractions

### Section 2.1: Understanding Fractions
A fraction represents a part of a whole. It is written as numerator / denominator (a/b), where denominator b ≠ 0.
- Proper Fraction: Numerator is less than denominator (e.g., 3/4).
- Improper Fraction: Numerator is greater than or equal to denominator (e.g., 7/5).
- Mixed Number: Contains a whole number and a proper fraction (e.g., 1 2/3).

### Section 2.2: Fraction Operations
1. Addition & Subtraction: Find the Least Common Denominator (LCD) before operating.
   Example: 1/3 + 2/5 = (5/15) + (6/15) = 11/15.
2. Multiplication: Multiply numerators together and denominators together.
   Example: (2/3) × (4/7) = (2 × 4) / (3 × 7) = 8/21.
3. Division: Multiply by the reciprocal of the second fraction.
   Example: (3/4) ÷ (2/5) = (3/4) × (5/2) = 15/8.

## Chapter 3: Decimals & Percentages

### Section 3.1: Decimal Operations
Decimals represent fractions with denominators of 10, 100, 1000, etc.
- Converting Fraction to Decimal: Divide numerator by denominator. Example: 3/4 = 0.75.
- Place Values: Tenths (0.1), Hundredths (0.01), Thousandths (0.001).

### Section 3.2: Percentages
A percentage is a fraction out of 100, represented by the symbol %.
- Formula: Percentage = (Part / Whole) × 100%
- Conversion: 25% = 25/100 = 0.25.
- Finding Percent of a Number: To calculate 15% of 80, compute 0.15 × 80 = 12.

## Chapter 4: Elementary Algebra & Linear Equations

### Section 4.1: Algebraic Expressions and Variables
A variable (e.g., x, y) represents an unknown numerical value.
An algebraic expression combines variables, constants, and operators (e.g., 3x + 5).

### Section 4.2: Solving One-Step and Two-Step Linear Equations
Goal: Isolate the variable on one side of the equation.
Rule of Equality: Whatever operation is performed on one side must be performed on the other side.

Example 1: Solve 2x + 6 = 14
Step 1: Subtract 6 from both sides: 2x = 8
Step 2: Divide both sides by 2: x = 4

Check: 2(4) + 6 = 8 + 6 = 14. Verified.
